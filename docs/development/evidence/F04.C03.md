## Expected
A nontechnical person can read what Alpha understood, answer a few questions by picking options or typing, see a plain-language brief with their choices marked, and correct it in their own words; nothing on screen asks about databases, schemas, components or layouts.

## Observed
- Request → "How Alpha understood it" (Outcome / Main input / Useful result), a one-sentence reply, two questions each with a one-line "why it matters", radio options plus "Something else:" free text, and "Use these defaults for now".
- Answers → "What Alpha will make: a reusable tool you can keep using", "How you'll use it" (three steps), "What it keeps for you" (food entries: eaten at, food, amount, calories), "Assumptions you can change" tagged default / you chose (2 + 4), "Not possible yet" (keeping your entries and history; its own screen), "Understanding 2".
- Free-text correction "Also track the water I drink each day" via "Change or add something" → the four "you chose" tags are kept, a new assumption is tagged "you corrected", the reply says everything else was kept, "Understanding 3"; "Start over" available throughout.
- Vocabulary: the captured page text contains no database, schema, SQL, JSON, API, component, column or framework terms (the only hits of a word scan are inside "editable"). Data kinds are shown as "a date and time", "text", "a number".
- Layout: one column at 768 (assistant above activity, no horizontal overflow), two columns at 1024 and 1440; the brief card scrolls inside its panel; no page errors in the console during the flow.
- Corroboration: apps/desktop/src/assistant/AssistantPanel.test.tsx (3 tests) asserts the same structure and the absence of technical vocabulary over the sample brief; the live model outputs in F04.C01 have the same shape with real text (interpretation, questions with options and why-it-matters, plain-language brief).
